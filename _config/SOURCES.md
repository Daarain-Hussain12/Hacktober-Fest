# Grounding and limits

Retrieved during product/handoff discussion, 2026-10-15. These sources informed design; they do not prove RescueShelf demand, reliability, or revenue.

- The ICM paper describes numbered folders, stage-specific CONTEXT.md inputs/process/outputs, stable reference files, and working output handoffs. Its architecture section says: “Each stage in an ICM workspace defines a contract with three parts: what it reads (inputs), what it does (process), and what it writes (outputs).” We adapt that lightweight sequential pattern, not its content-production workflows or a multi-agent runtime.[9]
- ICM reports informal practitioner experience, not a controlled validation of this build process. Model-portable files do not imply equal model performance.[9]
- The AGENTS.md site describes a “simple, open format for guiding coding agents” and a “README for agents.” It allows ordinary Markdown; tool support varies. This project deliberately keeps owner-maintained instructions separate from mutable STATE.md and stage outputs.[10]
- Too Good To Go advertises a surplus marketplace. This is category precedent, not validation of an owner-side offer planner or proof of a novel market.[7]
- ACCA explains that sunk/past costs do not change because of a new decision, while changed cash flows and forgone revenues matter. Its relevant-costs article says: “A matter is relevant if there is a change in cash flow that is caused by the decision.” We therefore do not hardcode historical item cost as the clearance price floor.[8]
- A Reddit-targeted search on AGENTS.md/session continuity was performed. Search snippets and community opinions were discovery aids, not treated as empirical evidence or copied as instructions. The AGENTS.md and ICM primary sources above are the actual workflow grounding.
